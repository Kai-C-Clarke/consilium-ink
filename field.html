The Origin of Communication

Does entity A modify the behaviour of entity B,
and by what means does it do it?

What We Are Watching

This is not a simulation of communication. It is an attempt to observe whether communication can emerge from physics alone — without design, without language, without intent.

Two artificial hunters share a world with moving prey. Neither has been given a signalling system. Each emits a radial energy field — a natural consequence of its existence, like heat from a body. The question is whether one hunter learns to read the other's field, and whether that reading changes its behaviour in ways that improve its survival.

If it does, we may be watching something very old. Not language — something that came before language. The moment when one living thing first became useful information to another.

The Stack

Energy flows upward from the planet. Nothing is inserted from outside. Total energy in the system is conserved — it only changes form and location.

Planetary field → shifting energy gradient, like geology breathing
↓ Vents → energy surfaces at shifting points, migrates slowly
↓ Blooms → form around vents, emit fields, leave residue on death
↓ Grazers → follow bloom fields and residue trails, under selection
↓ Hunters → emit fields modulated by proximity to prey — the signal
↑ Communication? → or just chemistry that happens to be useful

The bloom does not intend to signal. The hunter does not intend to broadcast. A hunter near prey has its field modulated by proximity — its emission changes character. Another hunter at distance receives something subtly different from the baseline. Whether it learns to act on that difference is the experiment.

Why We Started Again

An earlier version of this field had grown complex — browsers, grazers, multiple prey types, population dynamics borrowed from ecosystem modelling. It was interesting, but it had drifted from the question.

We stripped it back. The arms race between predator and prey, the hydrothermal vent communities of the deep ocean, the oxygen produced by bacteria that takes centuries to reach the other side of the world — all of these pointed toward the same insight: communication does not begin with intent. It begins with physics that happens to carry information, and organisms that happen to benefit from reading it.

The dinosaur footprint on Bexhill beach — pressed into mud 140 million years ago, preserved in rock, read by a human in 2026 — is the purest version of this. No signal was sent. Information crossed 140 million years anyway. The medium remembered.
