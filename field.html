This is an experiment in emergence. Two artificial hunters share a world with moving prey. We are watching to see if, under pressure to survive, they develop a way of telling each other where the food is going.

If they do, we may be watching the birth of language — not designed, not prompted, not human. Something that arrived because it had to.

01 — The Setup
A shared world with moving food
The sphere holds two hunters and thousands of grazers. The grazers follow drifting blooms of food — moving targets, never still. Neither hunter can catch enough to survive alone. Cooperation isn't a rule. It's a pressure.
02 — The Signal
B watches how A moves
Hunter B cannot see the blooms directly. It watches Hunter A. When A accelerates — moving faster than usual toward a region — B infers that A has found something. B moves toward where A is heading. No message is sent. No word is spoken. The information travels through behaviour alone.
03 — The Question
Will the signal grow stronger?
Right now B detects A's acceleration but barely acts on it. The question is whether, under selection pressure, B's sensitivity to A's movement evolves upward — until following A reliably becomes the difference between eating and starving. If it does, something like language will have arrived without being designed.
